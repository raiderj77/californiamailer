# Faceless sales research and operating rules

Last reviewed: 2026-08-18

## Definition

For CaliforniaMailer, faceless sales means **no-call-required and asynchronous**, not hidden ownership. An advertiser can review a factual campaign page and private concept, reply by email, reserve, pay through hosted checkout, submit materials, approve a versioned proof, and receive written delivery/tracking information without a meeting or on-camera pitch. A direct conversation with the owner remains available by request.

### Format and inventory are model-specific

Official High Response Marketing material supports several layouts, but it does not support treating every number of boxes as interchangeable:

- Its [9x12 design guidance](https://highresponsemarketing.com/part-iv-cheaper-design/) calls 14 a strong layout, prefers 16, and describes 18 as approximately the most that fits comfortably on a 9x12 or 8.5x11; it says a 12x15 can accommodate about 25. Therefore, **24 equal saleable ads on a 9x12 are not a verified layout assumption**. A 24-unit concept must pass a legibility and postal preflight with the exact artwork, address block, indicia, branding, disclosures, bleed, and safe areas before it can become inventory. If 24 conventional ads are required, the larger 12x15 format is the better-supported starting point.
- The official [M6 sample page](https://highresponsemarketing.com/samples/m6/) treats M6 as six ad spaces. It shows that splitting a space can create an M7 and that further splits can create M8 or M9 variants. Those variants are adjustable layouts, not evidence that every resulting unit has the same size, value, or price.
- The official [directory-card guide](https://highresponsemarketing.com/grant/) describes up to 72 small listings, with a few larger ads intended to cover production. Directory listings, premium listings, and display ads must be separate SKUs rather than counted as 72 conventional ad placements.
- [Community cards](https://highresponsemarketing.com/community/), [new-mover cards](https://highresponsemarketing.com/new-mover-marketer/), and the M3 system listed in the [QuickStart catalog](https://highresponsemarketing.com/courses/quickstart-lessons/) are distinct products. They differ in audience, timing, number and type of advertisers, targeting, fulfillment, and renewal logic; their pricing and inventory cannot be copied into the 9x12 model.

The website and owner calculator should therefore name the chosen model, physical size, stock, quantity, fulfillment path, layout capacity, paid slot units, and actual number of paid advertisers. A double-width placement consumes two units. Unsold, house, public-service, postal, or branding space produces no advertiser revenue and must not be silently counted as paid inventory.

Across those models, the reusable sequence is: choose the mail type and audience; document route, quantity, and current costs; preselect compatible advertisers; use short, truthful, permission-first written outreach; provide a factual sales sheet or private concept; collect written materials and payment through controlled gates; obtain proof approval; verify print readiness; authorize fulfillment manually; and report delivery and measured results. Email and direct messages must not become bulk spam or conceal the commercial sender.

## EDDM and supplier pricing sources

Pricing snapshot reviewed **2026-08-18**:

- USPS lists **EDDM Retail at $0.260 per piece** for USPS Marketing Mail flats up to 3.3 oz. The July 2026 Notice 123 became effective July 12, 2026: [USPS EDDM](https://www.usps.com/business/every-door-direct-mail.htm) and [July 2026 Notice 123](https://pe.usps.com/resources/PriceChange/July%202026%20Price%20Change%20-%20Notice123%20PDF%20-%207.2.26.pdf). EDDM BMEU is a different, entry-dependent product and must not be substituted for the Retail rate without verifying eligibility and entry conditions.
- The required print supplier is [Printing4SuperCheap](https://www.printing4supercheap.com/). High Response Marketing's official [printing-price link](https://highresponsemarketing.com/printing/) redirects to its [Discounted EDDM Printing4SuperCheap Pricing sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1y9xPkUy1xs6yOVj-bkOjM1apvZNMxm8CEnG6j1Y1Ynk/edit?gid=0#gid=0). The reviewed sheet covers multiple EDDM sizes and quantity tiers and states what its listed printing and optional turnkey charges include. Treat it as a dated supplier snapshot, not a permanent quote.

For the 9x12, 14 pt, full-color, UV-gloss option, the reviewed supplier sheet lists **$1,209 for 5,000** and **$2,299 for 10,000**, with shipping to the door stated as included. Its optional turnkey line is **$0.33 per piece**, described as including postage, preparation, direct shipment to the post office, and tracking, plus **$5 of banding per 1,000 pieces**. On those stated inputs, the known turnkey subtotal is:

| Quantity | Printing | Turnkey and banding | Known subtotal |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5,000 | $1,209 | $1,675 | $2,884 |
| 10,000 | $2,299 | $3,350 | $5,649 |

These are not complete campaign costs. Supplier tax, design and revisions, payment processing, refunds/disputes, reprint and contingency reserves, software allocation, owner labor, and any quote-specific exception remain separate inputs. A self-managed Retail-postage comparison is $2,509 for 5,000 and $4,899 for 10,000 before bundling, post-office delivery, tax, design, and the other costs above; do not treat that narrower subtotal as the safer fulfillment choice or as a final quote.

The owner planning target of **approximately $2,500** means pre-income-tax **owner economic surplus** after complete campaign costs and the selected owner-labor valuation. It is not guaranteed profit, cash available at an intermediate point, or $2,500 after personal income or self-employment tax. The auditable relationship is:

`required cleared net revenue = complete campaign costs + target owner economic surplus`

`required price per paid slot unit = required cleared net revenue / paid slot units`

Using only the dated turnkey subtotal and pretending every other cost is zero would produce misleading mathematical floors of about $225 per unit for 24 paid units at 5,000 pieces and $340 at 10,000. Because a 24-ad 9x12 layout is itself unverified and the omitted costs are real, those values must not be published as customer prices. The calculator must keep missing inputs visibly unknown, solve payment fees from gross revenue rather than subtracting a guessed flat amount, and show sensitivity for fewer paid units before recommending a price.

Before every customer quote, funding decision, checkout activation, or print authorization, reopen the USPS price source and supplier sheet and confirm the exact size, stock, quantity, shipping destination, turnaround, banding, number of destination post offices, postage, preparation, taxes, and other fees. Record the verification date and source alongside the estimate. If the public sheet, signed-in supplier account, or a direct written quote differs, stop and resolve the discrepancy before publishing a price or margin.

**Never reuse 2016 postage, printing, fulfillment, or profit examples from saved course PDFs or older videos.** The saved All-in-One PDF, for example, used 17.6-cent postage, quoted approximately $1,930 for a 5,000-piece campaign and $3,400 for 10,000, and illustrated 14 spaces at $500. Those figures are useful only for understanding the historical shared-cost workflow; they are not current costs, safe prices, expected margin, or evidence that the same layout will work. Historical examples may explain a workflow, but they must not populate current customer pricing, campaign economics, or margin gates. Do not rely on screenshots, cached snippets, downloaded spreadsheets, or hardcoded rates when the current USPS and supplier sources can be checked.

## Recommended five-touch sequence

1. **Day 0 — permission first:** 50–100 words, one verified personalization signal, exact territory/price/funding/refund basics, and “Open to reviewing the private concept?”
2. **Day 3–4 — private concept:** reply in-thread with a 45–60 second screen recording or annotated sample, clearly marked `CONCEPT — NOT APPROVED OR PUBLISHED`.
3. **Day 7–8 — fact sheet:** current real category status, target-versus-verified reach, funding rule, inclusions, and written policy link.
4. **Day 12 — optional physical proof pack:** only for the strongest qualified prospects; send a simple arrival note after confirmed delivery.
5. **Day 17–21 — close the loop:** state that follow-up will stop. Do not imply a reservation, deadline, or other advertiser unless the database confirms it.

Any reply pauses the cadence. Any opt-out suppresses all further marketing contact. Use only manually approved sends; no ringless voicemail or automated call sequence.

## Primary-source compliance guardrails

- The FTC says CAN-SPAM applies to business-to-business commercial email as well as bulk email. Sender/routing information and subject lines must be accurate; commercial messages need clear ad identification, a valid postal address, an easy opt-out, and prompt suppression: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- USPS says actual route delivery statistics come from its EDDM Online Tool. The roughly 5,000-household figure therefore remains a planning target until selected routes are documented: https://postalpro.usps.com/mailing/every-door-direct-mail
- The FCC treats ringless voicemail to consumer cell phones as a call subject to robocalling restrictions and prior-express-consent requirements: https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-389372A1.pdf
- California Penal Code section 632, reproduced by the California Attorney General, prohibits intentionally recording a confidential communication without all parties' consent in covered circumstances: https://oag.ca.gov/node/58179

This is an operating caution, not legal advice. Final outreach, recording, privacy, and contract practices require California counsel review before production use.

## Qualification gate

A prospect is not ready to contact until the owner records:

- official business source and current website;
- matching service area and campaign territory;
- category and conflict group;
- one current, verifiable offer or active-advertising signal;
- public business contact source;
- no do-not-contact or prior opt-out;
- no conflicting advertiser already held or paid.

Missing evidence stays `researching`, never silently becomes qualified.

## Trust assets required before outreach

- owner-managed positioning and real contact method;
- one founding-campaign page with target reach clearly separated from verified route count;
- exact proposed prices, inclusions, category rule, funding gate, and refund policy;
- sample card labeled as a concept;
- privacy, terms, funding/refund, and advertiser-content standards;
- secure hosted checkout only after campaign activation;
- written materials/proof/delivery workflow;
- valid postal address and opt-out method in commercial email.

## Scoreboard

Track qualified prospects, manual touches, delivery/bounce, replies, positive replies, holds, cleared payments, fees, refunds, complaints, proof approvals, delivery records, and owner hours. Keep web/QR events separate from advertiser-reported leads, appointments, and sales. Do not treat opens, clicks, scans, or testimonial earnings as revenue proof.
